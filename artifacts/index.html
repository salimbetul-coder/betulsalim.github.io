Project Charter

Proposed Lurentra pilot · Draft for discussion

Download Markdown ↓

PORTFOLIO WORKED EXAMPLE — Prepared September 2026. Based on Lurentra product context. Proposed plans, roles, ratings and simulated status are illustrative, not approved or historical project records.

Purpose: demonstrate how a business can turn a spreadsheet of sales and collections data into understandable metrics, with transparent validation and reliable outputs.

Context and authority

Known context: Lurentra is a B2B sales and collections analytics product. Betül’s documented responsibilities include scope, priorities, acceptance criteria and release readiness. This charter proposes a future pilot; sponsor approval, reviewer availability, budget and dates remain unconfirmed.

Pilot scope

  • In scope: one agreed input template; upload and column mapping; validation feedback; agreed sales and overdue-collections metrics; a management summary and CSV/PDF outputs.
  • Out of scope for this example: live ERP integrations, automated collection actions, multi-company rollout, custom model training and paid production onboarding.
  • Change control: record the request, business reason, delivery impact and acceptance changes. The project lead decides whether to replace existing scope or defer the request.

The founder holds project-lead, engineering and QA responsibilities in this example; these are separate responsibilities, not a claim of separate staff. Approval status

RACI Matrix

Proposed responsibilities · Same pilot scope as the charter

Download Markdown ↓

PORTFOLIO WORKED EXAMPLE — Prepared September 2026. Based on Lurentra product context. Proposed plans, roles, ratings and simulated status are illustrative, not approved or historical project records.

R = Responsible for doing the work; A = Accountable for the decision or result; C = Consulted; I = Informed. Each activity has one accountable role. A/R means that role both owns and performs the activity.

Roles and assumptions

  • PL: Project lead / founder — scope, priorities, decisions and communication.
  • ENG: Engineering responsibility — implementation and technical investigation.
  • QA: Validation responsibility — checks and release evidence.
  • BR: Business reviewer — proposed pilot participant; not yet confirmed.
  • For this solo-founder example, PL, ENG and QA are held by Betül. This is a responsibility model, not an organization chart or evidence of a staffed team. Business validation remains a proposed external dependency.

Review role assignments at kickoff and whenever scope or availability changes. Confirm the business reviewer before scheduling the pilot review. If no reviewer is available, record that limitation and hold the business-validation gate rather than claiming sign-off.

Review and escalation

  • Review open entries at each milestone and before the weekly update. Record evidence when closing an item.
  • An issue is an observed problem within the sample scenario; a risk is a possible future event. Keep them distinct.
  • If AC-01 to AC-04 cannot be evidenced, hold M2. If a critical blocker remains, hold M3. Do not mark a milestone complete to preserve the plan.
  • For this solo-founder model, escalation means an explicit scope, sequencing or go/no-go decision by the project lead, documented for the reviewer.
